# ehllo Domain Model

Status: Working domain specification  
Last updated: 2026-07-24

## Domain philosophy

ehllo follows one information lifecycle:

> **Raw → AI → Human → Permanent**

1. **Raw:** preserve what was captured or observed.
2. **AI:** generate disposable interpretations and proposals.
3. **Human:** accept, edit, or reject each meaningful proposal.
4. **Permanent:** write only reviewed information into durable relationship memory and actions.

Nothing generated by AI may silently become a trusted fact, alter identity, change a relationship, create a binding commitment, or send a message.

## Aggregate structure

```text
Workspace
├── Users
├── Cards
├── People
│   ├── Relationships
│   ├── Exchanges
│   ├── Encounters
│   │   └── AI Reviews
│   └── Actions
└── Inbox Items
```

Timeline is not a primary domain object. It is a chronological projection assembled from Exchanges, Encounters, approved review outcomes, Actions, relationship changes, and future Messages.

An implementation may maintain append-only timeline-event records for performance or auditing, but those records are projection infrastructure rather than the source of domain truth.

## Supporting tenancy boundary: Workspace

Workspace owns People, Relationships, Exchanges, Encounters, AI Reviews, Actions, Inbox Items, and Cards. The MVP creates one personal Workspace per User. Establishing the boundary now allows future collaboration without re-parenting relationship history.

## Object 1: User

### Purpose

An authenticated person who operates ehllo.

### Owns

- Authentication identity
- Display profile
- Personal and notification preferences
- Settings
- Subscription and Workspace memberships
- Card editing permission

### Does not own

People or relationship history. The Workspace owns them.

### Core attributes

`id`, `displayName`, `primaryEmail`, `avatarReference`, `locale`, `timeZone`, `status`, `createdAt`, `updatedAt`

### States

Invited, Active, Suspended, Deleted

### Invariants

- Authentication credentials are not stored on domain records.
- A User must belong to a Workspace to operate product data.
- Removing a User does not delete Workspace-owned relationship history.

## Object 2: Card

### Purpose

A deliberately public professional identity and exchange surface.

### Core attributes

`id`, `workspaceId`, `ownerUserId`, `publicSlug`, `displayName`, `photoReference`, `headline`, `company`, `publicLinks`, `contactMethods`, `exchangeSettings`, `status`, `publishedAt`, `createdAt`, `updatedAt`

### States

Draft, Published, Unpublished, Archived

### Invariants

- A Card contains no private relationship history, Encounters, AI Reviews, Actions, or Inbox Items.
- A public URL resolves only published Card data.
- Unpublishing does not delete historical Exchanges.

## Object 3: Exchange

### Purpose

An immutable record that a professional identity exchange happened.

### Core attributes

`id`, `workspaceId`, `cardId`, optional `personId`, `occurredAt`, `method`, optional `sourceReference`, `returnedDetails`, `consentState`, optional `coarseLocation`, `createdAt`

### Methods

QR, Public link, Manual entry, Business-card capture, Future integration

### Matching states

Unmatched, Matched, Possible duplicate, Intentionally separate

### Invariants

- Occurrence time, method, source Card, and original returned details are immutable.
- Corrections create identity or matching records rather than rewriting history.
- Anonymous Card views do not create People.
- Returned details require an explicit consent record.
- Precise location is not collected without a validated need and permission.

## Object 4: Person

### Purpose

Represents identity: who someone is.

### Core attributes

`id`, `workspaceId`, `displayName`, `givenName`, `familyName`, `company`, `role`, `emails`, `phones`, `linkedInUrl`, `otherIdentityLinks`, `labels`, `avatarReference`, `createdAt`, `updatedAt`, `archivedAt`

### Does not contain

Meeting notes, Encounter summaries, tasks, follow-up state, or relationship health.

### Invariants

- Identity changes are user-authored or user-approved.
- AI may propose identity corrections but never apply them silently.
- Explicit duplicate merging preserves source references and history.
- Deleting or merging cannot orphan Exchanges, Encounters, or Actions.

## Object 5: Relationship

### Purpose

Represents the Workspace's changing relationship with a Person.

### Core attributes

`id`, `workspaceId`, `personId`, `ownerUserId`, `stage`, `health`, `importance`, `desiredContactFrequency`, `lastMeaningfulContactAt`, `nextTouchAt`, `status`, `createdAt`, `updatedAt`

### Example stages

New, Prospect, Client, Partner, Advisor, Investor, Friend, Former

### Health values

Unknown, Healthy, Needs attention, Waiting, At risk

Health must never be presented as objective AI truth.

### Invariants

- One active Relationship exists per Workspace–Person pair.
- Stage and health changes become permanent only through user action or approved AI Review.
- Anonymous Card viewing does not change `lastMeaningfulContactAt`.
- Importance and frequency remain user-controlled.

## Object 6: Encounter

### Purpose

The raw record of a meaningful interaction.

### Core attributes

`id`, `workspaceId`, `primaryPersonId`, `participantPersonIds`, `createdByUserId`, `occurredAt`, `type`, `source`, `location`, `rawNotes`, `voiceReferences`, `photoReferences`, `attachmentReferences`, `explicitTopics`, `explicitPromises`, `captureStatus`, `processingStatus`, `createdAt`, `updatedAt`

### Types

Meeting, Call, Coffee, Conference, Message thread, Introduction, Other

### Sources

Manual capture, QR Exchange, Calendar, Email, WhatsApp, Voice memo, API or integration

### States

Draft, Saved, Processing, Review ready, Processing failed, Reviewed, Archived

### Invariants

- Original captured material is preserved.
- AI interpretation never overwrites raw notes.
- An Encounter can include multiple People.
- Saving never depends on AI availability.
- Attachments are private and access-controlled by default.

## Object 7: AI Review

### Purpose

A disposable, versioned proposal generated from an Encounter.

### Core attributes

`id`, `workspaceId`, `encounterId`, `modelReference`, `promptVersion`, `inputVersion`, `status`, `summaryProposal`, `factProposals`, `promiseProposals`, `questionProposals`, `actionProposals`, `reminderProposal`, `followUpProposal`, `relationshipUpdateProposal`, `confidenceWarnings`, `generatedAt`, `reviewedByUserId`, `reviewedAt`

### States

Queued, Processing, Ready, Failed, Superseded, Partially approved, Approved, Rejected

### Invariants

- Regeneration creates a new Review version.
- A Review never edits Person directly or sends communication.
- Approval records exactly what the User accepted or edited.
- Rejected or superseded Reviews cannot create permanent data.
- Model and prompt versions remain traceable.

### Approval effects

Approved output may create or update Actions, create reviewed Encounter memory, apply an explicit Relationship change, and create an Inbox Item. Every effect is attributable to the approving User.

## Object 8: Action

### Purpose

Represents durable work or a commitment.

### Core attributes

`id`, `workspaceId`, `personId`, `relationshipId`, optional `encounterId`, `createdByUserId`, `ownerUserId`, `type`, `title`, `details`, `status`, `dueAt`, `waitingUntil`, `completedAt`, `completionNote`, `createdAt`, `updatedAt`

### Types

Reminder, Task, Reply, Prepare, Schedule, Check-in, Follow-up

### States

Proposed, Open, Due, Overdue, Waiting, Completed, Dismissed, Cancelled

### Invariants

- AI-created Actions begin as Proposed.
- Opening an external email client does not complete an Action.
- Completion requires explicit confirmation or a future verified integration event.
- Completed Actions remain historical.
- Dismissal is distinct from completion.

## Object 9: Inbox Item

### Purpose

An ephemeral attention record explaining why something needs a decision now.

### Core attributes

`id`, `workspaceId`, `userId`, `reason`, `priority`, `decisionType`, optional `actionId`, optional `aiReviewId`, `personId`, `visibleFrom`, optional `expiresAt`, `state`, `createdAt`, `resolvedAt`

### Reasons

AI review ready, Action due, Action overdue, Follow-up approval required, Possible duplicate, Missing relationship information, Upcoming meeting preparation, Waiting period ended

### States

Active, Snoozed, Resolved, Dismissed, Expired

### Invariants

- Inbox Item is not an Action.
- Resolving it does not delete the referenced Action or Review.
- A resolved item disappears from the active Inbox but remains auditable.
- At least one reference explains the attention reason.
- Ordering is explainable through reason, priority, and time.

## Timeline projection

The Person timeline answers: “What happened in this relationship, in what order, and what changed?”

### Projection sources

- Exchange occurrence
- Encounter occurrence and review completion
- Action creation, state change, completion, or dismissal
- Relationship stage or health change
- Future reviewed Message events

### Rules

- Order by domain occurrence time, using creation time as fallback.
- Preserve and link to the originating object.
- Show whether information is raw, proposed, reviewed, or completed.
- Never expose private information on the public Card.
- The projection must be rebuildable from source objects.

If append-only `TimelineEvent` records are introduced, they cache or audit domain changes, point to a source object and version, and never become the sole source of truth.

## Relationships and cardinality

```text
Workspace 1 ── * User memberships
Workspace 1 ── * Card
Workspace 1 ── * Person
Workspace 1 ── * Relationship
Workspace 1 ── * Encounter
Workspace 1 ── * Action

Card 1 ── * Exchange
Person 1 ── * Exchange
Person 1 ── 1 active Relationship per Workspace
Person * ── * Encounter
Encounter 1 ── * AI Review versions
Encounter 1 ── * Action
Relationship 1 ── * Action
Action 1 ── * Inbox Item over time
AI Review 1 ── * Inbox Item over time
```

## Permanent memory boundary

Permanent relationship memory includes only user-authored Person identity, immutable Exchanges, original Encounter capture, user-approved summaries and facts, explicit or approved Relationship changes, approved Actions and their history, and confirmed communication outcomes.

It excludes unreviewed AI output, abandoned drafts, rejected proposals, anonymous Card-view assumptions, and automatically inferred completion.

## Deletion and retention

- Workspace deletion removes or anonymises owned data according to the final retention policy.
- User deletion must not silently destroy shared Workspace history.
- Person deletion requires review of linked history and may archive or anonymise where appropriate.
- Raw recordings and attachments should support shorter retention than reviewed structured memory.
- **Shared cloud recordings:** 3-day availability for guest play/download; daily cleanup removes Supabase objects while host local copies, full transcript, and shared summary remain.
- AI inputs and outputs require explicit retention rules and must not be retained indefinitely by default.
- Legal deletion and privacy rights take precedence over application-level immutability.

## Future integration contract

- **Calendar:** creates an Encounter draft or preparation Inbox Item.
- **Email:** links source material to Person or Encounter and may create an AI Review.
- **WhatsApp:** adds authorised source material to an Encounter.
- **Voice:** adds raw media and transcript versions to an Encounter.
- **Business-card scanning:** creates an Exchange, then enters Person matching and identity review.

No integration silently updates Person, completes Action, or bypasses human review.

## Expected persistence boundaries

- `users`
- `workspaces`
- `workspace_memberships`
- `cards`
- `exchanges`
- `people`
- `relationships`
- `encounters`
- `encounter_participants`
- `ai_reviews`
- `actions`
- `inbox_items`
- Optional `timeline_events` projection or audit table

Supporting tables for links, contact methods, attachments, review decisions, and state history should be normalised during database design. This document defines responsibility, not the final physical schema.
