# ehllo Customer Discovery Plan

Last updated: 2026-07-24

## Research objective

Determine whether independent consultants and fractional professionals repeatedly experience meaningful meeting-to-follow-up failures, whether current workarounds are unreliable, and whether the consequence is strong enough to motivate changed behaviour and payment.

The segment, problem, job, and 72-hour outcome are working hypotheses. Interviews do not validate them automatically.

## Hypotheses

- **HYP-001:** Independent consultants have at least three meaningful external professional conversations in a typical week.
- **HYP-002:** Important context and commitments are distributed across multiple tools or memory.
- **HYP-003:** The existing workflow causes missed, late, or generic follow-ups.
- **HYP-004:** The consequences are meaningful enough to motivate behavioural change.
- **HYP-005:** Current CRM tools, where used, are too burdensome for this workflow.
- **HYP-006:** Users are willing to capture encounter context within two hours.
- **HYP-007:** Reviewed follow-up assistance is more valuable than digital-card functionality alone.
- **HYP-008:** A 72-hour follow-up window is relevant to most valuable encounters.
- **HYP-009:** Users would pay for a product that reliably improves this workflow.

## Recruitment criteria

Participants should:

- Work independently or in a very small consultancy
- Sell high-value professional services
- Personally manage potential-client, partner, and referral follow-ups
- Have regular external professional conversations
- Include both CRM users and non-users
- Be recruited without presenting ehllo as the answer

Avoid collecting unnecessary personal or commercially sensitive information. Use reference IDs and pseudonyms where practical.

## Sample composition

Recruit at least ten completed interviews across:

- Product or design consultants
- Marketing or growth consultants
- Technology or engineering consultants
- Strategy or operations consultants
- Small agency owners

Include varied years of independent work, meeting frequency, workflow maturity, and CRM usage. The goal is explanatory range, not statistical representativeness.

## Interview guide

1. Tell me about the last important professional meeting you had with a potential client, partner, or referral.
2. What made that meeting important?
3. Immediately after the meeting, what did you do?
4. Where did you record what was discussed?
5. What did you or the other person promise to do?
6. How did you remember the next action?
7. When did you follow up?
8. Show me, where appropriate, the tools or notes you used.
9. Tell me about the last time a professional follow-up happened late or not at all.
10. What was the consequence?
11. How often does something similar happen?
12. What have you tried to prevent it?
13. Do you use a CRM? Why or why not?
14. Which part of the current workflow feels most frustrating or unreliable?
15. Have you paid for any tool intended to manage contacts, relationships, tasks, or follow-ups?
16. What caused you to continue or stop using it?

## Consent guidance

- Explain the research purpose and how notes will be used.
- Confirm participation is voluntary and questions may be skipped.
- Ask explicit permission before recording.
- Record the consent state in the interview record.
- Store only the minimum information needed for research.
- Do not paste confidential client details, credentials, health data, financial data, or unrelated personal information.

## Evidence-quality rules

- Prefer recent, specific behaviour over general opinion.
- Prefer demonstrated workflows and artefacts over recalled claims.
- Separate participant statements, demonstrated behaviour, and researcher interpretation.
- Record contradictory evidence with equal care.
- Do not treat repeated mentions as proof without considering evidence type and strength.
- Do not ask “Would you use this?” or other leading solution questions.
- Hypothesis status is always a manual judgement.

## Synthesis process

1. Complete the interview record within 24 hours.
2. Tag each relevant evidence entry to one or more hypothesis IDs.
3. Mark whether the evidence supports or contradicts each hypothesis.
4. Compare interviews using concise qualitative descriptions.
5. Update repeated pains, workflows, failure points, alternatives, consequences, payment signals, contradictions, segment differences, and open questions.
6. After five interviews, review the guide and sample for blind spots without declaring validation.
7. After at least ten completed interviews, prepare the decision gate.

## Decision criteria

The researcher manually chooses one outcome:

- Proceed
- Revise segment
- Revise problem
- Run more research
- Stop

The decision record must include an evidence summary, strongest supporting evidence, strongest contradictory evidence, required changes, and date. Ten completed interviews unlock the review; the interview count alone never validates the segment or problem.
